# Setu: video walkthrough script (about 3 minutes)

**Before recording**
- Open the live demo and click **Reset demo** (top right) so the data is fresh.
- Use a desktop browser at full width. Zoom to 110% if text looks small.
- Record the screen with OBS, Loom or the Windows Snipping Tool's screen recorder. Record the voice-over separately if that's easier, then combine.
- Speak at a calm pace (about 140 words per minute). Each scene's narration fits its time slot.

---

### Scene 1 · 0:00–0:15 · Title (deck slide 1, or the app header)

> In one South Indian study, thirty-four percent of cervical cancer patients who completed treatment were lost to follow-up. Often the reasons aren't medical: bus fares, scheme paperwork, family duties. We're Team MedNova, and this is Setu.

### Scene 2 · 0:15–0:35 · Worklist tab

**On screen:** scroll the worklist; hover over the priority formula.

> Every morning, Setu gives the care team a ranked list of patients who have missed a planned visit. The ranking is operational, not clinical: days overdue, times a follow-up tier our doctor sets for each step, times past misses. No diagnosis, no risk model. And it needs no EMR integration, just the hospital's appointment export.

### Scene 3 · 0:35–0:55 · Verify register tab

**On screen:** point to Sunil Shinde's red phone field, type `9765301128`, click **Approve row**. Point to Parvati Kale's future date.

> Many day-care units still use handwritten registers. Setu digitises only six non-clinical fields, never drugs or doses, and a clerk checks every row. Here, the OCR dropped a digit from the phone number, and here it misread a date into the future. Setu blocks the row until a person fixes it.

### Scene 4 · 0:55–1:50 · Outreach agent: Shanti Devi

**On screen:** Worklist → **Start outreach** on Shanti Devi → **Send heads-up** → **Place call** → tap the caregiver reply (Raju, her son) → tap **1974** → tap the Ayushman card / bus fare reply → pick a slot.

> Let's bring back Shanti, fifty-two, chemo cycle four, nine days overdue, a hundred and forty kilometres from the hospital.
> First, a WhatsApp heads-up from the hospital care desk, with a neutral sender name, so a message on a family phone never reveals the diagnosis.
> Then a voice call in Hindi. Her son answers. Before discussing anything, Setu confirms identity with her birth year.
> Then one question: is anything making it difficult to come?
> *"The Ayushman card expired, and the bus fare is too much."*
> Setu recognises the barriers, scheme paperwork and travel cost, sends the exact document checklist and low-cost lodging options on WhatsApp, and offers a vacant chemo slot. Booked, and confirmed on WhatsApp. The coordinator just sees a one-line summary.

### Scene 5 · 1:50–2:15 · Safety: call drop and emergency card

**On screen:** start outreach for the next patient (e.g. Lakshmi Narayanan, English). Place call → **Simulate call drop** to show WhatsApp voice notes. Reset demo, start Lakshmi again: verify, then tap **"I have had fever since yesterday"**. Open the **Nurse queue** tab.

> If the call drops, Setu continues on WhatsApp with voice notes, or by SMS with a missed-call number for feature phones.
> And Setu never gives medical advice. Here, "fever since yesterday" matches the hospital's own emergency card, so Setu reads the hospital's instruction word for word and alerts the on-call nurse immediately. Any other symptom goes to the nurse queue, and the nurse decides.

### Scene 6 · 2:15–2:40 · Barrier dashboard, then Pathways & safety

**On screen:** dashboard bars and suggested action; then change one tier on the Pathways tab and flick back to the Worklist.

> For the first time, the hospital sees why its patients drop out. Here, scheme paperwork leads, so the fix is a help-desk change, not another reminder.
> Pathways and tiers belong to the clinician. Change a tier, and the worklist re-ranks instantly. The same engine already runs a high-risk pregnancy schedule.

### Scene 7 · 2:40–3:00 · Close (deck slide 8 or the app footer)

> Setu is assistive by design: no diagnosis, no treatment advice, no risk scoring. Next, we connect Sarvam voice, Exotel and WhatsApp, and run a sixty-to-ninety-day pilot measuring how many overdue patients return to care within thirty days.
> Setu: no patient falls through the gap.
